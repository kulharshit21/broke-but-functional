# 1. UNDERSTAND THE CORE PROBLEM

## Problem Statement

Build a modern, interactive personal finance experience for Gen Z that helps users manage a limited ₹10,000 budget over 30 days and make informed financial decisions.

The application must go beyond basic expense tracking by connecting:

- Income and available cash
- Current account balance
- Daily and cumulative spending
- Essential and discretionary expenses
- Upcoming financial commitments
- Recurring subscriptions
- Savings goals
- Unexpected expenses
- Future cash-flow projections
- Spending behavior
- Financial risk
- Overall financial health

Users must be able to simulate financial decisions before making them and clearly understand how those decisions affect their remaining balance, daily allowance, savings goals, monthly forecast, financial risk, and overall financial health.

### The fundamental product philosophy

**Every financial decision has consequences, and those consequences must be immediately visible.**

If a user spends ₹1,500 on a spontaneous night out, the application must not simply subtract ₹1,500 from the balance.

It must also show:

1. The change in spendable cash.
2. The reduction in safe daily spending allowance.
3. Whether upcoming bills remain affordable.
4. How the projected month-end balance changes.
5. Whether their savings goal becomes harder to achieve.
6. Whether the risk of running short of money increases.
7. How their financial health score changes.
8. What adjustments could help recover.

These values must come from **one consistent financial calculation engine** rather than separately hardcoded dashboard metrics.

## Numerical accuracy

Use integer paise internally to avoid floating-point errors when handling money.

Format displayed currency using Indian formatting conventions and the ₹ symbol.

Examples:

₹10,000

₹1,249

₹199.50

Round only at well-defined display or conversion boundaries.

### Distinguish the following concepts

Do not mix these up:

**Current balance:** Actual money currently in the spending account.

**Upcoming commitments:** Bills not yet paid.

**Reserved savings:** Money intentionally allocated to savings rather than freely spendable cash.

**Safe-to-spend amount:** The portion of money reasonably available after future obligations and planned reserves are considered.

**Projected balance:** The future expected balance calculated from scheduled income, expenses, and estimated spending.

**Total cash:** Spending-account money plus savings balances where relevant.

A savings transfer should reduce the spending account but increase the saved balance. It must not incorrectly destroy the user's total money.

A bill should not be counted twice as both a paid expense and an unpaid future commitment.

A cancelled subscription must not continue affecting future projections beyond its effective cancellation date.

A scenario should not mutate actual state until it is explicitly applied.

# 7. FINANCIAL CALCULATIONS

Implement meaningful, explainable formulas.

## A. Remaining budget

Calculate the amount actually available after relevant obligations and allocations.

## B. Safe daily allowance

A useful initial approach:

Safe daily allowance = max(0, spendable cash after remaining essential commitments, planned savings allocations, and safety buffer) / remaining days

Include expected income only according to its timing and an explicitly defined forecast policy.

Do not allow the algorithm to assume future income can pay a bill due before that income arrives.

Handle periods where cash is insufficient with a clear warning.

## C. Daily cash-flow forecast

Generate a date-by-date projected balance through the end of the challenge.

For each day, account for:

- Actual transactions
- Future scheduled income
- Upcoming commitments
- Subscription billing
- Savings contributions
- Estimated discretionary expenses
- Applied hypothetical events

Ensure each event is counted exactly once.

## D. Spending pace

Compare actual cumulative discretionary spending against the available planned spending envelope for elapsed days.

Explain overspending or underspending without misrepresenting essential commitments.

## E. Burn rate and runway

Calculate recent average variable spending and a useful estimated runway.

Use a clearly documented deterministic heuristic such as a recent seven-day spending average with a reasonable fallback for insufficient history.

Avoid claiming to use predictive AI or machine learning unless an actual working model is implemented.

## F. Financial Health Score

Create a transparent score between 0 and 100.

It should consider clearly defined factors such as:

- Ability to cover future essentials
- Projected minimum cash balance
- Spending pace
- Available safety buffer
- Savings progress relative to plan

Use documented weights, bounded normalization, and edge-case handling.

Show users why their score changed.

A change that worsens the overall financial position should not arbitrarily improve financial health without a defensible offsetting factor.

## G. Risk detection

Detect issues such as:

- Projected negative balance
- Unaffordable upcoming essential payments
- A dangerously small safety buffer
- Excessive discretionary spending pace
- Subscriptions consuming an unusually large share of spendable cash
- Savings contributions that are no longer feasible

Assign understandable warning levels.

Do not invent statistically precise bankruptcy probabilities or fake AI confidence percentages.

Financial risk labels should be supported by explicit thresholds and deterministic forecasts.
